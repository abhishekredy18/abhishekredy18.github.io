## Problem

Large-scale deployment of autonomous vehicles requires robustness to
unstructured traffic and adverse weather, and must prevent unsafe
mispredictions. Existing segmentation benchmarks focus on well-organized,
controlled settings — not the combination of bad weather and unstructured
driving conditions, and not safety.

## Approach

We built IDD-AW, a benchmark of 5000 pairs of high-quality images with
pixel-level annotations, captured under rain, fog, low light, and snow in
unstructured Indian driving conditions — from the highways of Hyderabad to
the snowy hills of Manali and the foggy roads of Delhi and Ooty. Compared
to other adverse-weather datasets, IDD-AW provides more annotated images, a
paired Near-Infrared (NIR) image for every frame, and a larger label set
with a 4-level label hierarchy that captures unstructured traffic.

## Architecture & Tech

High-resolution RGB and NIR camera sensors captured over 1 million frames;
rigorous quality checks distilled these into the final 5000 RGB-NIR pairs,
annotated for semantic and instance segmentation. We benchmarked
state-of-the-art segmentation models on the dataset.

We also proposed "Safe mean Intersection over Union (Safe mIoU)", a metric
for hierarchical datasets that penalizes dangerous mispredictions the
traditional mIoU definition does not capture.

Alongside the benchmark, I built SB3-based reinforcement learning models in
SUMO to optimize lane selection and vehicle-to-vehicle communication,
reducing emergency vehicle traversal times.

## Results

The results show IDD-AW is one of the most challenging segmentation
datasets to date. The work was published at the IEEE/CVF Winter Conference
on Applications of Computer Vision (WACV), January 2024.
