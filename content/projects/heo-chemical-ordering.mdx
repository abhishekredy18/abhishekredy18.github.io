## Problem

Understanding chemical ordering in high-entropy oxides requires sampling
configurations across temperatures — but every energy evaluation with
density functional theory (DFT) is expensive, making direct simulation at
meaningful scales infeasible.

## Approach

I trained equivariant graph neural network potentials (Allegro, NequIP) on
DFT data to predict atomic-scale energies and forces, building ML
surrogates that replace quantum-mechanical calculations at a fraction of
the compute cost.

## Results

The trained models serve as the energy engine for Monte Carlo simulations
of HEO-J14, enabling sampling of chemical ordering across temperatures at
scales infeasible with direct DFT.
